TOPIC 1.1 — MEANING OF CONSTITUTION


Flashcards


# Front (Question) Back (Answer)
1 General meaning of "Constitution"? Written, precise, systematic document; supreme law; special amendment procedure
2 Who can draw up a Constitution? Constituent Assembly, legislature, or King binding himself and successors
3 What happens to statutory law inconsistent with Constitution? Unconstitutional/ultra vires when court reviews it
4 British Constitution: defined or undefined? Undefined, unsystematised, undecided; lacks precision and coherence
5 Does one book contain the British Constitution? Many books describe it, but no one book contains it
6 Most important part of British Constitution? What is kept out of written law; given to sole guardianship of custom
7 Constitutional law and statutory law in Britain? Stand at par; same source; same amendment procedure
8 Can courts set aside Parliament's enactments in Britain? No; no judicial review
9 Jennings on British Constitution? "Not made but grown—and there is no paper"
10 Paine's view? Where Constitution "cannot be produced in a visible form, there is none"
11 Tocqueville's view? In England Constitution "may go on changing continually or rather it does not exist"
12 Were Paine and Tocqueville correct? No; no State without a constitution
13 No constitution is wholly ___ or entirely ___? Written; unwritten
14 Bryce on written constitutions? "Developed by interpretation, fringed with decisions, enlarged by customs"
15 US Constitution: Articles and pages? 7 Articles; about 10 pages
16 Indian Constitution: Articles and Schedules? 395 Articles; 12 Schedules
17 Difference between written and unwritten constitution? One of degree, not kind
18 No constitution is complete by itself—what is it? A framework/skeleton filled out with detailed rules and practices
19 What supplements constitutional provisions? Amendments, customs, judicial decisions
20 Wherever there are rules determining creation/operation of governmental institutions, there exists? A constitution


One-Line Revisions


1. Constitution = written, precise, systematic, supreme law, special amendment.
2. Britain = no single document; undefined, unsystematised, scattered.
3. Constitutional law = statutory law in Britain; no judicial review.
4. Greater part of British Constitution = unwritten, carried in men's minds.
5. Jennings: "Not made but grown—no paper."
6. Paine/Tocqueville wrong: no State without constitution.
7. No constitution wholly written or entirely unwritten.
8. Bryce: written constitutions grow by interpretation, decisions, customs.
9. US: 7 Articles/10 pages; India: 395 Articles/12 Schedules.
10. Written vs unwritten = degree, not kind.
11. No constitution complete; it's a skeleton filled by rules/practices.
12. Where there are rules for governmental institutions, there is a constitution.
